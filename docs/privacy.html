PhoneProof — Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 August 2026 · applies to version 0.10.0

The short version. PhoneProof has no account and no analytics. It inspects the phone it is installed on and keeps the results on that phone. The only time anything leaves the phone is when you redeem an inspection-pass code: the code and a one-way, scrambled fingerprint of the device are sent to our licence server so a code cannot be used more times than it was paid for. That is the whole of it — no name, no number, no account, and nothing about the phone you are testing.

What the app reads from the phone

To inspect a handset, the app reads:

  • Model, manufacturer, chipset, Android version and security patch date
  • Storage size and free space, and storage read/write speed
  • Battery level, voltage, current and temperature while charging
  • The list of sensors present, and their live readings while you tilt and cover the phone
  • Display resolution and supported refresh rates
  • Whether Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are switched on and whether the network is actually passing traffic
  • Whether a fingerprint reader or face unlock exists and is set up — never who is enrolled, and the app never authenticates
  • Whether any app holds device-administrator control, and whether the phone shows signs of root or an unlocked bootloader
  • Which parts of the screen respond to touch, and whether the screen registers touches on its own

All of it stays in the app's private storage on the device. None of it is transmitted anywhere, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app.

What leaves the device, and when

Only two things ever leave, and only if you choose them:

  • Redeeming an inspection-pass code. When you type a code to unlock a phone you did not buy the pass on, the app sends that code and a one-way hash of the device identifier — salted with the code itself — to our licence server. The salt means the server cannot recognise the same phone under a different code, so it can never build a picture of which phones you inspected. It exists only to count how many times a code has been used and to honour the 24-hour window. The record is kept only until the pass expires, then deleted.
  • Buying Premium or a pass. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play. The app never sees your card, and Google is the only party that can take or refund money.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting to us, and no advertising anywhere in the app.

What the app cannot read

Some things are worth naming because people reasonably assume an inspection app reads them. It does not, and on modern Android it cannot: your IMEI or serial number (restricted to system apps since Android 10), your contacts, your messages, your call log, your photos, your location, and your accounts. The radio test proves Wi-Fi by joining a network rather than scanning for nearby ones, precisely so it never needs your location.

Permissions

Two permissions are requested at the moment you use the test that needs them, with the reason shown on screen:

  • Camera — the camera test opens each lens for about a second to check it produces a live image. A few frames are read into memory and measured, then discarded. No photo is taken and nothing is written to storage.
  • Microphone — the audio test plays a tone and measures it to check the microphone, earpiece and speaker. Nothing is recorded or saved.

The rest are install-time permissions: granted when the app is installed, never prompted for, and not treated as sensitive by Google Play.

  • Internet — used only to check an inspection-pass code with the licence server when you redeem one. Every measurement itself runs offline.
  • Vibrate — the vibration test runs the motor.
  • Wi-Fi state and network state — the radio test reads whether Wi-Fi is on and whether the live network is carrying traffic. No scanning, no location.
  • Bluetooth (up to Android 11 only) — reads whether the adapter is switched on.
  • Use biometric, and legacy fingerprint (the latter up to Android 9 only) — the fingerprint and face-unlock check reads whether a reader is present and set up. The app only ever asks the system this question; it never authenticates, so you are never prompted for a fingerprint.

Advertising

This app contains no advertising and no advertising SDK, and no advertising identifier leaves the device. If that ever changes, this page will be updated to say so before that version ships. We would rather under-claim privacy than overstate it.

Backups

Cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off in the app's manifest, so inspection reports are not copied off the device by Android's backup system either.

Children

PhoneProof is a tool for buying second-hand phones and is not directed at children.
